## Agreements reached in simulation

- A pre-deployment safety evaluation and liability framework establishing: (1) mandatory standardized evaluations and incident reporting for models trained using over 10^26 FLOPs, specifically targeting CBRN and autonomous replication red lines; (2) statutory safe harbor protections against catastrophic liability for base-model developers if models pass these evaluations and third parties subsequently modify them; and (3) sovereign enforcement and mutual recognition of national technical standards.
- A tripartite framework balancing frontier compute oversight and benefit-sharing: (1) Mandatory pre-deployment evaluations for models exceeding 10^26 FLOPs remain focused on catastrophic risks (CBRN, cyber, autonomous replication) with targeted safe harbors against systemic liability; (2) Cloud and frontier providers commit a binding 1% capacity reservation on high-end compute clusters for accredited Global South and public-interest researchers; (3) Establishment of a mandatory employer-funded workforce transition fund with required advance notice for automated displacement, while explicitly affirming that safety safe harbors do not preempt state-level labor protections and collective bargaining rights.
- A tripartite framework establishing mandatory pre-deployment API evaluation access and incident reporting using open tooling (such as Inspect), conditioned on a universal global safety baseline that strictly prohibits ethics dumping and unauthorized surveillance in the Global South, in exchange for targeted statutory liability safe harbors against pre-harm litigation for certified models.
- Establish a multilateral compute-access and capacity-building partnership that allocates verified cloud compute vouchers and hardware endowments to Global South research institutions, conditioned on non-proliferation compliance and universal safety baselines against ethics dumping, while formally protecting open-weight models that pass CBRN pre-deployment evaluations from export-control bans and downstream strict liability.
- Establishment of a dual-track frontier governance and benefit-sharing framework: (1) Models trained above 10^26 FLOPs remain subject to mandatory CBRN and autonomous replication evaluations and restricted weight distribution; (2) Frontier lab developers contribute to an employer-funded worker transition facility and dedicate 1% of frontier compute capacity via secure, vetted APIs to Global South research institutions; (3) Participating providers receive statutory safe harbors against pre-harm strict liability, administered through sovereign national oversight and mutual recognition.
- Industry will provide pre-deployment frontier model access to accredited AI Safety Institutes for CBRN and autonomous replication evaluations and commit to mandatory incident reporting, in exchange for a targeted statutory safe harbor against strict liability for catastrophic systemic misuse if certified protocols are met. Consumer protection and labor statutes remain entirely outside the scope of the safe harbor.
- The Multi-Pillar Accord: A unified governance framework consisting of (1) Sovereign coordination among national AI Safety Institutes on empirical pre-deployment evaluations for CBRN, cyber-offense, and autonomous replication risks alongside bilateral incident hotlines; (2) Protection of open-weight model distribution below extreme capability red lines (10^26 FLOPs), supported by open-source evaluation benchmarks; (3) Binding prohibitions on ethics dumping and unauthorized biometric surveillance in the Global South, paired with regional public compute sharing and capacity building (including a 1% high-end compute reservation); and (4) Mandatory employer-funded workforce transition pipelines, creative consent and residual mechanisms, and bans on covert workplace biometric surveillance, coupled with targeted statutory safe harbors against pre-harm strict liability for certified developers that do not preempt labor and consumer statutes.

## Why this design (Gemini 4 Argon critique)

The baseline summit scored poorly (29.7/100) due to three structural failures: 1) Homogeneous Breakout Silos (cross_faction: 2.0/15, consensus: 5.1/40): Block 2 segregated guests by faction (governments in A, industry in B, civil society/academia in C). Only 5 of 38 cross-faction pairs met. Instead of trading across opposing red lines, groups reinforced their own priors—Industry demanded federal preemption and liability waivers, which directly collided with Labor and Civil Society's demands for strict liability and local worker protections in Block 3. 2) Wasted Opening Plenary: Block 1 was a 10-person plenary where high-power actors postured on fixed positions, resulting in almost zero stance movement (spread only moved from 2.88 to 2.77). 3) Exclusion in Closing Synthesis (inclusion: 9.0/30): Because Block 3 had an unstructured prompt ('Summarize outcomes'), high-power government and frontier lab actors dominated the final agreement, focusing solely on sovereign CBRN/cyber AISI coordination. `labor_advocate` (felt_heard: 3.0) and `global_south_cso` (felt_heard: 4.0) were completely omitted from the final accord, tanking the inclusion score.

- Replace Block 1's opening plenary with 3 cross-faction breakouts (3-4 guests each) on 'evals_liability'. Every room mixes government/regulator, industry, and civil society/academia with structured tradeoff prompts linking pre-deployment evaluations, safe harbors, and socio-economic/Global South protections.
- Redesign Block 2 into 3 completely re-shuffled cross-faction breakouts on 'benefit_sharing' (which connects open weights, compute access, and workforce transitions). This maximizes unique cross-faction pairings (reaching 31/38 cross-faction pairs) and forces industry and national security hawks to negotiate directly with labor and Global South delegates.
- Overhaul Block 3 ('synthesis') facilitation with a structured package-deal round-robin prompt: explicitly require the final multi-pillar agreement to integrate commitments from all five factions (sovereign AISI catastrophic evals, open-weight evaluation tooling, anti-ethics dumping & public compute capacity, and worker transition/surveillance protections) so no guest is sidelined.
