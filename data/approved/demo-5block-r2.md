## Why this design (Gemini 4 Argon critique)

The Four-Pillar Cross-Faction Matrix raised the score from 28.9 to 71.6/100, achieving a perfect 15.0/15 on cross_faction (38/38 pairs met), 14.2/15 on airtime, and 8 cross-faction agreements. However, two specific bottlenecks limited consensus (21.4/40) and inclusion (21.0/30, min_heard = 7.0):
1. Three red-line friction points capped inclusion at 7.0 for open_weights_lead, big_tech_policy, and global_south_cso: (a) Our Block 2 and 3 prompts framed open-weight protections around 'sub-frontier (<10^26 FLOPs) exemptions,' which triggered open_weights_lead's red line against prohibiting the distribution, distillation, or fine-tuning of frontier open-weight models; (b) big_tech_policy (7.0) and labor_advocate were in separate rooms in Block 1, so federal preemption of state technical standards (preserving state labor laws) was omitted from the final B5 agreement text; and (c) global_south_cso (7.0) conceded on real-time public biometric surveillance bans because us_natsec demanded broad domestic surveillance exemptions rather than narrowing exemptions strictly to military/DoD procurement.
2. Incomplete stance convergence on regulation, open_weights, compute_controls, and benefit_sharing (spread_after = 2.49 vs. 2.88): (a) Block 1's prompt focused on pre-deployment evaluations and tort safe harbors, which moved 5 delegates on evals_liability but only 1 delegate (big_tech_policy) on regulation; (b) In Block 2 (compute_controls), three compute hawks (safety_researcher +4.0, frontier_lab_ceo +3.5, uk_aisi +3.0) were grouped together in Room 2, pushing frontier_lab_ceo away from the center (+3.5 to +4.5); (c) In Block 3 (open_weights), Room 1 had three negative delegates (-4.5, -3.5, -2.0) while Room 2 had two neutrals (0.0, 0.0) and only one negative (-4.0), so neither open_weights_lead (+5.0) nor frontier_lab_ceo (-4.5) moved; and (d) In Block 4 (benefit_sharing), frontier_lab_ceo (+2.5) was grouped with labor_advocate (+5.0) and uk_aisi (+3.5), moving up to +3.5 instead of toward the center.
