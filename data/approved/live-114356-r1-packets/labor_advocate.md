# Labor Union Technology & Workforce Transition Leader (`labor_advocate`)

Today, your objective as the Labor Union Technology & Workforce Transition Leader is to secure binding statutory floors that protect state-level worker laws from federal preemption, outlaw covert workplace biometric/keystroke surveillance, and mandate human-in-the-loop appeal rights and creative consent/residuals. In exchange for union algorithmic audit rights, employer-funded retraining pipelines, and extended severance, you are prepared to support assistive AI deployment and structured safety standards.

## Breakout Openings

### Block 1: 09:00-10:00 | Breakout A | Pre-Deployment Evaluations & Liability Tradeoffs
**With:** eu_regulator (EU AI Office), us_natsec (U.S. National Security Advisor for AI), big_tech_policy (VP of Public Policy, Major Cloud & AI Platforms)

- Offer `big_tech_policy` and `eu_regulator` union support for assistive AI deployment and internal operational model training (with union algorithmic audit rights), in exchange for preserving stronger state-level worker protections against federal preemption and banning covert workplace biometric/keystroke surveillance.
- Propose to `eu_regulator` and `big_tech_policy` that mandatory pre-deployment evaluations and Article 91 transparency disclosures explicitly cover automated hiring, disciplinary, and termination systems—guaranteeing human-in-the-loop oversight and a formalized right to appeal.
- Ask `us_natsec` and `big_tech_policy` to commit that national security and commercial regulatory streamlining will never override collective bargaining agreements, state labor floors, or explicit consent and fair compensation for worker-generated training data.

### Block 2: 10:15-11:15 | Breakout C | Benefit Sharing, Compute Access & Open Innovation

- Ask `big_tech_policy` to drop demands for broad federal preemption of state labor and civil rights laws, trading instead on a narrow technical safe harbor for pre-deployment AISI-tested models that strictly forbid covert workplace surveillance.

## Know your counterparts

- **eu_regulator (EU AI Office)**
  - *Top Red Line:* Refusal by general-purpose AI providers to comply with binding Article 91 Requests for Information (RFIs)
  - *What They'd Trade:* Willing to act as a neutral facilitator and delegate working-group leadership to independent chairs to crowdsource industry expertise, provided market actors respect backstop statutory compliance and deterrence.
- **us_natsec (U.S. National Security Advisor for AI)**
  - *Top Red Line:* No international treaties or regulatory constraints that compromise U.S. technological advantages or limit rapid military AI adoption.
  - *What They'd Trade:* Willing to engage in discrete bilateral channels and crisis-management mechanisms (such as a U.S.-China incident hotline) to mitigate automated nuclear or cyber escalation without signing binding arms-control agreements.
- **big_tech_policy (VP of Public Policy, Major Cloud & AI Platforms)**
  - *Top Red Line:* A 50-state patchwork of un-preempted state AI legislation.
  - *What They'd Trade:* Offering advanced frontier models to government AI Safety Institutes for pre-deployment testing in exchange for federal preemption of state regulations.
- **uk_aisi (UK AI Safety Institute (AISI) Directorate)**
  - *Top Red Line:* Deployment of any model exhibiting unmitigated autonomous cyber-offense capabilities, chemical/biological weapon synthesis (CBRN), or the intentional subversion of human oversight (ASL-3/ASL-4 limits).
  - *What They'd Trade:* Conceding full model weight access for interpretability in exchange for robust API and chat-interface access to maintain developer cooperation.
