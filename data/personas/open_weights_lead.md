

## Ecosystem Allies and Points of Friction
The open-weights policy posture relies heavily on diverse, cross-sector coalitions to legitimize its arguments. 

| Stakeholder Relationship | Key Entities involved | Nature of the Dynamic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategic Allies** | AI Alliance, Open Source Initiative, Linux Foundation, Enterprise Infrastructure Providers (e.g., NVIDIA, Dell). | Unified by the desire for vendor-neutral infrastructure and transparent codebases. Hardware providers benefit from broad hardware usage; enterprise customers benefit from avoiding vendor lock-in [cite: 9, 15, 19, 21, 39]. |
| **Primary Friction Points** | Frontier Model Forum (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google DeepMind). | Open-weights leaders publicly accuse these rivals of weaponizing safety concerns and "AI alarmism" to secure monopolistic moats and stifle competition through regulatory capture [cite: 11, 13, 14, 40, 41]. |
| **Secondary Friction Points** | National Security Agencies and Defense Regulators. | Tension arises when regulators attempt to apply traditional arms-control logic (like the Defense Production Act or export controls) to digital weights, which open-weight leaders argue undermines domestic sovereignty [cite: 27, 42]. |

These dynamics were highly visible during the publication of the "Open Weights and American AI Leadership" open letter, which mobilized over 270 signatories to push back against proposed restrictions on model distillation and weight distribution, actively isolating closed-model laboratories in the public policy discourse [cite: 9, 43, 44].

## Multistakeholder Engagement Dynamics
In global multistakeholder forums—such as the Bletchley Park and Seoul AI Safety Summits, the U.S. AI Safety Institute Consortium (AISIC), and the U.S. Senate AI Insight Forums—open-weights leaders utilize a collaborative but highly operational engagement style [cite: 45, 46, 47, 48]. 

Rather than adhering to the catastrophic risk framing dominant in executive closed-door sessions, they pivot discussions toward tangible "open innovation," data provenance, and practical trust-and-safety tooling [cite: 45, 49, 50]. They frequently establish and leverage formalized industry collectives, most notably the AI Alliance, to operationalize their policy stances. Through these vehicles, open-weights leaders engage in technical working sessions by donating open-source evaluation tools, testing frameworks, and risk taxonomies [cite: 50, 51, 52]. This ensures that emerging international safety standards and compliance mechanisms are designed to accommodate the decentralized realities of open-source distribution, rather than being tailored exclusively for proprietary API access [cite: 50, 52].
