

## Engagement Dynamics: Facilitation vs. Enforcement

The AI Office demonstrates a bifurcated engagement methodology, adopting distinctly different postures depending on the operational forum. 

In multistakeholder working sessions, such as the drafting of the GPAI Code of Practice, the Office deliberately adopts the posture of a neutral "facilitator" rather than an authoritative driver [cite: 35, 36]. By structuring plenary sessions with over 1,000 industry, academic, and civil society participants, and delegating leadership to independent chairs, the Office crowdsources technical expertise while fostering industry buy-in and minimizing adversarial pushback [cite: 8, 27, 35].

Conversely, in its formal regulatory capacity, the Office projects uncompromising prosecutorial authority. Official statements, published guidance, and enforcement notices are utilized to assert jurisdiction, issue binding requests for information (RFI) under Article 91, and establish rigid compliance deadlines [cite: 37, 38, 39]. The Office uses these formal channels to remind market participants of its sanctioning powers—which include fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover—ensuring that its collaborative posture during drafting phases is backed by credible, centralized enforcement deterrence [cite: 37, 39].
